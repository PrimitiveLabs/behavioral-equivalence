# Behavioral model card

Intended use: research on finite predictive abstractions and model-conditional verification. These models are not validated for production decisions, psychological diagnosis, individual risk assessment or arbitrary interface counterfactual prediction. No trained weights are included in the permitted release package.

Thirty context-only, GRU and Transformer fits cover both evaluation protocols and five fixed seeds (20261015 through 20261019). Each model has a 128-dimensional hidden representation. Inputs are available bundle-rate history, current tab, training-vocabulary item identity and declared intrinsic-duration context. User identity, current/future feedback, randomized-assignment flags and full-period response/popularity statistics are excluded as predictors. The context-only baseline removes behavioral history while retaining comparable current-exposure information.

The recorded parameter counts are 1,053,425 for context-only, 1,171,185 for GRU and 1,341,169 for Transformer in the fixed architecture. Training uses three fixed epochs, AdamW and validation checkpoint selection; the original configuration and later five-seed extension are retained separately. Current-context differences, learning objective and parameter counts mean this is not a pure causal ablation of human memory.

Training-only quantizers produce 16, 32, 64, 128 and 256 parent states. Independent quantizations are separate predictors, not quotient certificates of the 256-state model. Certified quotients preserve observable labels and bind actual transition rows. Human decision dynamics use outcome-conditioned transitions and a three-stage pending/negative/positive graph. H decisions correspond to 2H-1 graph transitions. A finite learned chain does not establish human Markov sufficiency.

The fixed primary strict abstraction retains 256 states in all ten fits and fails the twofold-compression target. Neither fixed human response study satisfies all-five strict improvement. Conservative 256-state emission intervals are wholly vacuous. These negative results are retained. Supplementary feedback/watch-time heads and sampled joint-trajectory comparisons are exploratory extensions, not replacements for primary decisions.

Supplementary heads use frozen encoders and validation-selected categorical logistic regularization. Their nominal certificates concern one endpoint emission; they do not certify the joint law of different feedback types. Joint click-trajectory scoring instead uses a coherent emission/transition model and sums hidden future paths. Direct marginal horizon heads have no joint sequence distribution. Frozen-origin iid comparisons are explicitly weak joint baselines.

Full primary aggregate losses and primary user-bootstrap intervals have independent output replay. All sampled joint-trajectory likelihoods and paired intervals have a separate backward replay. Supplementary endpoint audit is tracked independently. These audits do not independently retrain encoders or establish that the human-generating process belongs to a confidence set.
